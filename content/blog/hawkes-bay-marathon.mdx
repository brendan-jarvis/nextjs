I used Claude Code to plan the Hawke's Bay marathon. The plan lived in Notion. It had the twenty weeks, the long runs, the flights, and the Airbnb. I followed it. On 16 May 2026 I ran the ASICS Runaway Hawke's Bay Marathon, bib 479, and finished in 3:20:03.

That was the floor of the plan. The page said sub-3:20, with 3:15 as the stretch, and a start at 4:40 per kilometre, reassessed at 30 km. If it was going badly, settle for 3:20. The clock stopped three seconds the other side of that line. 151st of 1,127, 136th of 695 men, 36th of 140 in the 30–34 category. Net time.

The plan was a document, not a coach. Claude Code wrote the weeks and the trip. Notion was the thing I followed: Wellington to Napier on the Friday, a place in Greenmeadows within a walk of the start, the shakeout, the early dinner, the 8:20 am start. It did not message me on the morning.

The first 5 km went through in 20:10, about 4:02 per kilometre, quicker than the 4:40 the page asked for. By 21 km the average was 4:34. From 37 to 40 km the split slowed to 5:27, then the last 2.2 km came back to 4:28. The whole race was about 4:44 per kilometre. The written instruction was to decide at 30 km. The finish says the decision was the floor.

I would hand it the week structure again, and the travel. Flights and an Airbnb are a list, and a list is what it is good at. The race plan held. The part I would still not hand over is the feeling at 30 km. The page can tell you to reassess. It cannot tell you which way.

The result page is on SportSplits, under bib 479.
