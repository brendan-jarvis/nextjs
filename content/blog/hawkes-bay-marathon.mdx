I asked Claude Code to plan the ASICS Runaway Hawke's Bay Marathon. It wrote two plans. I followed the one in my calendar. Neither of those is what I ran.

This is not the right way to do this. A coach, or a published plan you actually do, is better. If you are going to use a model, treat the output as a draft and keep one schedule. I ended up with two, and then a third one on the watch.

## What it wrote

On 22 December 2025 Claude Code wrote `hawkes-bay-marathon-training.ics` and I imported it to iCloud. Every event was a 7:00am placeholder. The sessions were Wednesday, Saturday, and Sunday, around a gym split of Monday chest and biceps, Tuesday legs, Thursday shoulders and triceps, Friday back and abs.

That calendar is the plan I was looking at. Easy pace was written as 5:30–5:45/km early and 5:20–5:40/km later. Marathon pace on the workouts was 4:43/km. The race event said 3:19:00 at a constant 4:43/km.

It also wrote a Notion page, [Hawke's Bay Marathon Plan](https://www.notion.so/30893a670e2480118f2bd0d3bc8e24ad), last edited 15 February 2026. I was not following that page. It is shorter than the calendar, and several of its dates are one day off. Round the Bays is Sunday 15 February on the watch and in the result box, and Sunday 16 February in the week header.

The Notion page is the nicer document. It has the flights (NZ5882 out of Wellington at 11:30am on Friday 15 May, NZ5895 back at 4:45pm on Sunday), Relax on Ryan in Greenmeadows, the $1,030–1,080 budget, and a pacing card: start at 4:40/km, reassess at 30 km, 3:15 as the stretch, 3:20 as the floor. Every 5 km was supposed to be 23:20 for the first 30 km.

The two plans are not the same workouts with different numbers. Early quality sessions are different types. The calendar sums to 874 km. The Notion page sums to 755 km. The watch says I ran 601 km.

## What I actually ran

The watch is the source of truth. Apple Health export, 29 December 2025 through 17 May 2026. A row counts as a run if moving pace is faster than 7:30/km. The gap is clean: the slowest run is 5:52/km and the fastest excluded recording is 8:42/km.

Forty-one runs, 600.6 km. Another 17 recordings are saved as Running and are not runs. Cadence on those is often in the 30s and 40s. Several of them sit on a Wednesday where the calendar had a fartlek, hills, tempo intervals, or a marathon-pace session. I have no idea what I was doing with the watch on. I was not doing the session.

The long runs that did happen are closer to the calendar than to Notion, until late March.

- 1 March: 28.04 km at 5:15/km, then a 1.16 km jog that evening.
- 8 March: 28.20 km at 5:26/km.
- 15 March: 28.03 km at 5:29/km.
- 22 March: 22.01 km at 5:04/km.

Then the plan and the watch split.

The 30 km on 29 March is an 8.01 km walk-pace recording. The 34 km on 5 April has no workout. On 7 April there are two treadmill runs, 17.32 km and 13.08 km, on a day with nothing scheduled. On 12 April the last long run is 21.20 km on a treadmill, against 26 km on the calendar and a final 10 km at 4:50/km that did not happen.

Melbourne was the same shape. The calendar had easy runs on 22, 25, and 26 April totalling 21 km, and a note saying 15–20 km was enough. I ran 7.80 km on 19 April and 9.27 km on 22 April. Nothing on 25 or 26 April. The Notion page was more forgiving about this week than the calendar was. The page was right.

Most of the Wednesday speed work never happened. The fitness that did get banked was easy running, three 28 km long runs, and Round the Bays.

Round the Bays, Sunday 15 February. Watch: 21.19 km, 1:29:32, 4:13/km, average heart rate 172, max 179, 330 W, cadence 168. The Notion page has the official result as 1:29:08, 99th of 2,027. The result page says 1:29:25, 99th of 2,079. The page also records a February 2025 half of 1:31:00 and an August 2024 marathon of 3:47:16, which it describes as a training run. That half is the session the plan was built around. The rest of the quality work was mostly a description of sessions.

Week 15 was the biggest week I actually ran, 63.6 km, and it was not the week the calendar called the peak. Week 13 was 12 km against 60 km on the calendar. Week 14 was 12 km against 64 km.

## The race

ASICS Runaway Hawke's Bay Marathon, Saturday 16 May 2026, Anderson Park, Greenmeadows, gun 08:20, bib 479. Chip time 3:20:03. Place 151 of 1,127, 136th man of 695, 36th of 140 in the men's 30–34 group. The 42.2 km checkpoint row on the same page shows position 135, so the place line and the checkpoint line do not agree. Use 3:20:03.

Watch: 42.22 km in 3:20:24, 4:45/km, average heart rate 166, max 178, 295 W, cadence 163, 172 m of ascent, 10°C.

The calendar goal was 3:19:00. The Notion floor was 3:20. I finished three seconds over the floor and 63 seconds over the calendar.

I did not run the plan.

The first 5 km was 4:02. The page said 4:40, and it said the danger was adrenaline making 4:40 feel slow. It was right about the danger and I did not follow it. The half was 1:36:07, which is faster than the 1:38:30 the page wanted, because the first 10 km was so fast and the 15 km segment was so slow. I do not have a lap file that explains that 25:57. The split is the split.

From 32 km to 40 km the pace went to 5:01, 4:59, and 5:27. The page wanted a decision at 30 km: push toward 3:15 if it felt easy, hold 4:40 if it felt controlled, drop to 4:43 if it hurt. I was already behind 4:40 by 32 km. The last 2.2 km was 9:50, at 4:28. To break 3:20 that split needed to be 9:47. Three seconds.

## If you are going to do this

Do not keep two plans. I had a Notion page I was not reading and a calendar I was not completing, and I could not tell you on a Wednesday which of them was the session. Pick one. Put it where you will see it on the morning of the run. Delete the other.

Tell the model your actual week, not the week you want. Three runs around a lifting split is a real constraint. A 12 km tempo on Wednesday is not a real session if Tuesday was legs and you are going to leave the watch recording a walk. The Wednesday quality day is where this plan failed. The long runs, until the peak, are where it held.

Do not let it write the peak week as the week you will suddenly do 34 km. I had already done three 28 km runs. The next step did not happen. A plan that assumes the longest run of the block will be the one you complete is a plan written by something that does not get tired.

The pacing card was the useful part. Start at 4:40, decide at 30 km, 3:20 as the floor. I ignored the start and still landed on the floor, off a half marathon of 1:29 and a block that was mostly easy running. The floor was the right number. The first 5 km was not.

The model is fine at the document. Flights, the Airbnb, the shakeout, the gel count, the 23:20 chunks. It is bad at noticing that you have stopped doing the plan. I did not ask it again after December. By March the calendar was a record of sessions I was not going to do, and I kept looking at it anyway.

Run the one on the watch.
